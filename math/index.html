Linear Algebra 2 · Eigenvalues

A matrix is a transformation of the plane

The matrix A sends the basis vector e₁ to its first column and e₂ to its second. Drag the two colored arrow tips to edit the matrix. The gold lines are eigen-directions: vectors on them are only stretched, never turned.

[
]
Why this works

λ is an eigenvalue of A exactly when det(A − λI) = 0, which for a 2×2 matrix is λ² − tr(A)·λ + det(A) = 0. When the discriminant is negative there are no real eigen-directions, so every vector turns (try “Rotate 45°”). When det(A) = 0 the plane collapses onto a line.

Infi 2 · Gradient & directional derivative

Which way is uphill?

The colors show the height of a surface z = f(x, y) and the thin lines are level curves. Drag the white point, then turn the direction u. The slope in direction u is Duf = ∇f · u, which is largest when u points along the gradient.

Slope Duf as you turn u through 360°.
Why this works

By Cauchy–Schwarz, ∇f · u = |∇f| cos φ, where φ is the angle between u and ∇f. So the steepest ascent is at φ = 0 with rate |∇f|, and u is tangent to the level curve (slope 0) at φ = ±90°. This is why the gradient is always perpendicular to level curves, and the reason Lagrange multipliers work.
